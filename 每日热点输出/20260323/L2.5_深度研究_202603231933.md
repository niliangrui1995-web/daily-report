There are so many bottlenecks in the AI cluster value chain. GPUs. CoWoS. HBM. And today, our focus is on another one: lasers.

Electro-absorption modulated lasers (EMLs) are the component inside optical transceivers that converts electrical data into light. It’s a laser and a high-speed modulator on a single indium phosphide chip, and every 800G and 1.6T transceiver needs them.

Lumentum makes more of these lasers than anyone, somewhere between 50-60% of the global market. Yet demand outstrips supply by roughly 30%!

## Optical Platform & Integration Plays [...] An 800G optical transceiver typically uses four optical lanes. A 1.6T module uses eight. Each lane needs its own laser and photodetector, most of which are InP-based. So when the industry moves from 800G to 1.6T, InP consumption per module nearly doubles.

Now layer in AI cluster design. Platforms from Nvidia push toward rack-level bandwidths of several terabits per second. That means hundreds of optical modules per rack. Multiply that across thousands of racks per hyperscaler, and suddenly one company’s deployment can consume millions of InP laser components per year.

This isn’t linear growth. It’s architectural amplification.

Multiple independent forecasts point to 2026 as the year when these constraints become most visible. Demand continues to surge as hyperscalers standardize 1.6T optics and experiment with co-packaged optics. Supply, however, expands slowly.
